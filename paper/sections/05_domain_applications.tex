\subsection{Weather and Climate Forecasting}
Global numerical weather prediction represents the flagship proving ground for scientific foundation models. As demonstrated by Pangu-Weather~\cite{bi2023pangu}, GraphCast~\cite{lam2023graphcast}, FuXi~\cite{chen2023fuxi}, FengWu~\cite{chen2023fengwu}, and Aurora~\cite{bodnar2024aurora}, deep learning architectures execute 10-day global forecasts in tens of seconds on a single GPU, delivering computational speedups of $1{,}000\times$ to $10{,}000\times$ compared to operational NWP supercomputing workflows. Beyond raw speed, AI foundation models achieve superior deterministic skill across geopotential height ($Z500$), temperature ($T850$), and surface wind fields. Furthermore, versatile foundation models such as ClimaX~\cite{nguyen2023climax} demonstrate multi-task transferability by pretraining on synthetic CMIP6 climate projections and fine-tuning for regional climate downscaling and seasonal teleconnections.

\subsection{Sub-Kilometer Convective-Scale Emulation and Generative Downscaling}
\label{sec:convective_downscaling}
A fundamental limitation of global foundation models (operating at $0.25^\circ \approx 25\text{--}31\text{ km}$ or $0.1^\circ \approx 10\text{ km}$) is their inability to resolve sub-kilometer convective phenomena. Convective thunderstorms, microbursts, urban flash flooding, and complex orographic wind channeling occur at localized scales ($< 3\text{ km}$) where non-hydrostatic atmospheric dynamics dominate. Bridging this multiscale gap has motivated frontier research into generative radar nowcasting and residual diffusion downscaling:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Generative Radar Precipitation Nowcasting: DGMR}: Ravuri et al.~\cite{ravuri2021dgmr} established the landmark \textbf{DGMR} (Deep Generative Model of Radar), deploying spatial-temporal generative adversarial networks for precipitation nowcasting at 1 km spatial and 5-minute temporal resolution across the United Kingdom. Traditional deep learning baselines optimized with mean squared error (MSE) or mean absolute error (MAE) suffer from progressive spatial blurring and severe underestimation of intense convective rain cells due to multimodal trajectory averaging. DGMR overcomes this through dual-frequency spectral discrimination: a spatial discriminator enforcing realistic high-frequency radar precipitation textures, and a temporal discriminator penalizing physically implausible advective jumps. In rigorous double-blind evaluations conducted with professional operational forecasters at the UK Met Office, DGMR was ranked first in 89\% of cases for overall value, physical plausibility, and localized heavy rain cell fidelity compared to optical flow (PySTEPS) and convolutional recurrent baselines.
    \item \textbf{Continental Multi-Sensor Convective Forecasting: MetNet-3}: Andrychowicz et al.~\cite{andrychowicz2023metnet3} scaled convective forecasting across the entire continental United States (CONUS) out to 24-hour lead times at 1--4 km spatial resolution. Operating over a continuous spatial grid ($4{,}096 \times 4{,}096\text{ km}$), \textbf{MetNet-3} assimilates heterogeneous and asynchronous observational modalities directly: high-resolution ground radar composites (MRMS), geostationary satellite multi-spectral imagery (GOES-16), surface meteorological station networks, and numerical forecast initializations (HRRR). Employing axial multi-scale self-attention and spatially localized lead-time conditioning, MetNet-3 outperforms operational High-Resolution Rapid Refresh (HRRR) numerical physics models on 24-hour precipitation and surface temperature forecasts, producing complete continental updates every two minutes on TPU hardware.
    \item \textbf{Kilometer-Scale Residual Generative Downscaling: CorrDiff}: Bridging coarse global foundation model outputs ($25\text{ km}$ ERA5) to local convective scales ($2\text{ km}$), Mardani et al.~\cite{mardani2023corrdiff} formulated \textbf{CorrDiff}, a residual corrective diffusion framework developed for extreme weather prediction over complex topography (Taiwan). Rather than applying standard super-resolution networks that produce smooth hallucinations, CorrDiff decomposes downscaling into two physically distinct stages: (1) a deterministic regression mean conditioned on coarse atmospheric states, and (2) a score-based residual diffusion sampler that generates fine-scale stochastic fluctuations consistent with local topography and radar observations. CorrDiff accurately reconstructs the high-wavenumber power spectral density (PSD) of radar reflectivity, faithfully capturing the extreme heavy precipitation tails during landfalling typhoons while delivering a $1{,}000\times$ inference speedup and $3{,}000\times$ energy reduction relative to operational numerical WRF physics simulations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Earth Observation and Remote Sensing Time Series}
Satellite constellations continuously monitor terrestrial ecosystems, forest biomass, crop phenology, and surface water regimes through high-dimensional spatio-temporal revisit series. Foundation models in this domain must overcome three core physical challenges: (1) multi-sensor sensor heterogeneity (interleaving optical multispectral reflectance with active Synthetic Aperture Radar microwave backscatter), (2) irregular temporal acquisitions caused by dynamic cloud occlusion and orbital revisit periods, and (3) multi-scale spatial ground sampling distances (GSD) varying across orders of magnitude ($0.2\text{ m}$ to $500\text{ m}$).

\subsubsection{Temporal-Spectral Masked Autoencoding}
Early foundation models focused on optical and multispectral acquisitions. SatMAE~\cite{cong2022satmae} introduced temporal-spectral masked autoencoding, grouping Sentinel-2 and Landsat bands into physically coherent spectral groups (visible, near-infrared, and shortwave infrared) and masking spatio-temporal tokens independently. Presto~\cite{tseng2023presto} designed a lightweight, pixel-level transformer that processes sparse temporal series of Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 optical, and ERA5 weather variables without computing expensive spatial attention, delivering high efficiency for global crop classification. Advancing multi-scale representation learning, Galileo~\cite{tseng2025galileo} integrated multi-sensor satellite imagery across local and global scales with self-supervised masking, while AlphaEarth Foundations~\cite{brown2025alphaearth} compressed multi-sensor Earth observation into dense 64-dimensional neural embedding fields integrated into planetary cloud analytics. Scaling parameter capacity to multi-modal constellations, SkySense~\cite{guo2024skysense} unified optical, SAR, and DEM time series with 1.9 billion parameters, achieving state-of-the-art results across the GEO-Bench~\cite{lacoste2023geobench} suite.

\subsubsection{Contrastive Radar-Optical Masked Autoencoding: CROMA}
While optical sensors provide rich chemical and spectral reflectance signatures, they are blinded by cloud cover and darkness. Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), emitting microwave pulses (e.g., Sentinel-1 C-band VV/VH polarizations), penetrates clouds and operates day and night, capturing surface dielectric constants and geometric roughness. To fuse these complementary physical regimes, Fuller et al.~\cite{fuller2023croma} established \textbf{CROMA} (Contrastive Radar-Optical Masked Autoencoder).

CROMA unifies self-supervised contrastive learning and cross-modal masked reconstruction. Let $\mathbf{X}_{\text{opt}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B_{\text{opt}} \times H \times W}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sar}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B_{\text{sar}} \times H \times W}$ represent spatially aligned optical and SAR image pairs. CROMA applies separate modality encoders $\mathcal{E}_{\text{opt}}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{\text{sar}}$ with high masking ratios ($>70\%$), aligning global representations via a symmetric InfoNCE loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{contrast}} = -\frac{1}{2N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( \log \frac{\exp(\mathbf{z}_{\text{opt}}^{(i)} \cdot \mathbf{z}_{\text{sar}}^{(i)} / \tau)}{\sum_j \exp(\mathbf{z}_{\text{opt}}^{(i)} \cdot \mathbf{z}_{\text{sar}}^{(j)} / \tau)} + \log \frac{\exp(\mathbf{z}_{\text{sar}}^{(i)} \cdot \mathbf{z}_{\text{opt}}^{(i)} / \tau)}{\sum_j \exp(\mathbf{z}_{\text{sar}}^{(i)} \cdot \mathbf{z}_{\text{opt}}^{(j)} / \tau)} \right).
    \label{eq:croma_contrast}
\end{equation}
Simultaneously, a cross-modal transformer decoder predicts masked patch pixels by fusing optical and SAR tokens. Crucially, to overcome the rigid position-embedding limits of standard Vision Transformers, CROMA introduces \textbf{2D-ALiBi} (2D Attention with Linear Biases) and \textbf{X-ALiBi} (cross-attention ALiBi):
\begin{equation}
    \text{Attention}_{i, j} = \text{softmax}\left( \frac{\mathbf{q}_i \mathbf{k}_j^T}{\sqrt{d}} - m \cdot \|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j\|_2 \right) \mathbf{v}_j,
    \label{eq:2d_alibi}
\end{equation}
where $m > 0$ is a head-specific geometric slope and $\|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j\|_2$ denotes the Euclidean distance between 2D patch centers on the Earth's surface. By substituting static position embeddings with continuous distance penalties, CROMA performs \textbf{zero-shot spatial extrapolation} to satellite scenes up to \textbf{17.6$\times$ larger} than its training crops ($120 \times 120$ to $504 \times 504$ and beyond) without performance degradation, setting new records on the BioMassters canopy height benchmark and EuroSAT classification.

\subsubsection{Universal Multi-Resolution Earth Observation: AnySat}
Resolving the acute spatial resolution fragmentation across satellite constellations, Astruc et al.~\cite{astruc2024anysat} introduced \textbf{AnySat}. Rather than locking input tensors to fixed grid sizes (e.g., $10\text{ m}$ for Sentinel or $30\text{ m}$ for Landsat), AnySat formulates Earth observation foundation modeling across arbitrary spatial resolutions spanning three orders of magnitude ($0.2\text{ m}$ aerial orthophotos, $10\text{ m}$ Sentinel-2, to $500\text{ m}$ MODIS).

AnySat is built on a scale-adaptive Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA). Each input patch $i$ from sensor $s$ is explicitly conditioned on its continuous physical ground sampling distance $\text{GSD}_s$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}_i = \mathbf{W}_s \mathbf{x}_i + \boldsymbol{\psi}_{\text{scale}}(\log \text{GSD}_s) + \boldsymbol{\psi}_{\text{spatial}}(\mathbf{p}_i),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\text{scale}}$ is a multi-frequency sinusoidal scale embedding. Pretrained on the massive GeoPlex corpus (unifying 11 distinct spaceborne, airborne, and elevation sensors), AnySat achieves zero-shot multi-sensor fusion, allowing arbitrary combinations of available sensors at test time (e.g., substituting missing high-resolution aerial imagery with Sentinel-2 and SPOT) and outperforming single-sensor foundation baselines across land cover segmentation, agricultural crop mapping, and post-disaster flood inundation.

\subsubsection{3D Generative Spectral Foundation Models: SpectralGPT}
While RGB satellite foundation models capture basic spatial geometry, they disregard the continuous physical spectral dimension critical for mineral exploration, vegetation biochemical traits, and marine algae blooms. Danfeng Hong et al.~\cite{hong2024spectralgpt} introduced \textbf{SpectralGPT}, the first 600-million parameter foundation model tailored specifically for spectral remote sensing big data.

SpectralGPT is built upon a novel 3D Generative Pretrained Transformer (3D GPT) that performs 3D token generation across both spatial coordinates $(x, y)$ and continuous spectral wavelengths $\lambda$. In contrast to 2D vision models that flatten spectral bands into static channel dimensions, SpectralGPT treats spectral channels as an ordered physical continuum. The model employs a progressive pretraining curriculum across input images of variable ground sampling distances (GSD), spatial patch sizes, and multi-temporal revisit intervals. Pretrained on over \textbf{one million spectral scenes} spanning airborne hyperspectral cubes (AVIRIS, HySpex) and spaceborne multispectral images (Sentinel-2), SpectralGPT captures fine-grained spectral absorption signatures via multi-target masked autoencoding. Across downstream benchmarks, SpectralGPT delivers decisive improvements in single- and multi-label scene classification, semantic land-use segmentation, and change detection, setting a new benchmark for spectral geoscience applications.

\subsubsection{Global 10m Canopy Height and Carbon Stock Inversion}
Accurate estimation of global vegetation vertical structure is fundamental to resolving terrestrial carbon sinks and conserving planetary biodiversity. Dedicated spaceborne LiDAR missions (such as NASA's Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation, GEDI) deliver ultra-accurate canopy height waveform profiles, but only sample sparse footprint tracks ($25\text{ m}$ diameter spots separated by $60\text{ m}$ along-track). Conversely, optical satellite constellations (Sentinel-2) provide dense, continuous global coverage at $10\text{ m}$ ground sampling distance, but cannot directly penetrate forest canopies to measure vertical height.

Lang et al.~\cite{lang2023canopy} resolved this sparse-to-dense inversion challenge by establishing the first \textbf{wall-to-wall 10m-resolution global canopy height model of the Earth} for the year 2020. The authors formulated a probabilistic deep learning convolutional ensemble that ingests multi-temporal Sentinel-2 optical composites and predicts pixel-level canopy height $h(\mathbf{x})$ alongside heteroskedastic predictive uncertainty $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$. By learning non-linear spectral and textural features conditioned on sparse GEDI top-of-canopy elevation profiles, their architecture substantially alleviates the signal saturation effect that causes conventional remote sensing models to underestimate tall canopies ($> 30\text{ m}$). Their global inventory revealed that only $5\%$ of the Earth's landmass is covered by trees taller than $30\text{ m}$, and merely $34\%$ of these high-carbon stocks lie within designated protected areas, providing an operational tool for forest carbon accounting and conservation decision-making.

\subsection{Hydrology and Extreme Flood Modeling}
Hydrological forecasting requires predicting water discharge through river networks subject to precipitation forcing, evapotranspiration, and soil infiltration. A long-standing grand challenge has been ``Prediction in Ungauged Basins'' (PUB), where historical streamflow calibration records are absent. Nearing et al.~\cite{nearing2024globalflood} demonstrated that deep learning models trained on 5,680 diverse river basins across the globe achieve unprecedented zero-shot flood forecasting skill in ungauged catchments, extending actionable early warning lead times from 0 to 5 days globally and outperforming the Copernicus GloFAS operational physics-based system. Complementing these architectural advances, Kratzert et al.~\cite{kratzert2023caravan} released Caravan, a standardized global community dataset spanning 6,830 catchments worldwide with unified ERA5-Land meteorological forcings, catchment attributes, and daily streamflow records. By eliminating the geographic fragmentation of regional datasets (e.g., CAMELS-US, CAMELS-GB), Caravan establishes the empirical data substrate required to train and benchmark universal hydrological foundation backbones capable of cross-continental generalization.

\subsubsection{Hierarchical Multi-Agent Decisional Systems for Urban Flood Extremes (H-J)}
While basin-scale streamflow models (e.g., GlobalFlood, Caravan) predict macro-scale river discharge, extreme convective storms inflict sudden, localized urban flash floods. In densely populated metropolitan centers, effective emergency response requires coordinating multi-objective operational interventions: dynamically rerouting municipal traffic away from inundated underpasses, adjusting pumping station sluice gates, and dispatching rescue teams.

Addressing this multi-scale challenge, Ji et al.~\cite{ji2025entropy} established \textbf{H-J}, a hierarchical multi-agent framework integrating frontier LLMs with domain Knowledge Graphs (KG) and entropy-constrained strategy optimization. H-J orchestrates a closed-loop hierarchy:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Perception Agent}: Ingests streaming in-situ hydrological sensor networks, radar nowcasts, and urban road network topologies into dynamic spatio-temporal graphs.
    \item \textbf{Strategic Decision Agent}: Employs domain knowledge graphs of flood response protocols to restrict the LLM's generative search space, eliminating semantic hallucination and execution inconsistency.
    \item \textbf{Entropy-Constrained Optimizer}: Uses information-entropy constraints to balance competing operational objectives (maximizing overall traffic throughput while guaranteeing zero civilian strandings in submerged nodes).
\end{enumerate}
Evaluated on real-world urban topologies and hydrodynamic flood simulations under extreme rainfall surges, H-J achieves a $28.4\%$ increase in urban traffic network smoothness and a $34.2\%$ improvement in emergency task completion rate over standard rule-based dispatchers and reinforcement learning baselines, establishing the viability of multi-agent LLM systems for physical disaster mitigation.

\subsubsection{Spaceborne GNSS-Reflectometry and Multimodal Inundation Inversion}
While spaceborne optical sensors (e.g., Sentinel-2, Landsat) provide fine spatial resolution, they suffer catastrophic data loss under dense cloud cover, heavy monsoonal precipitation, and thick vegetation canopies---the exact meteorological conditions accompanying severe floods. Conversely, spaceborne Global Navigation Satellite System Reflectometry (\textbf{GNSS-R}), spearheaded by the NASA Cyclone Global Navigation Satellite System (\textbf{CYGNSS}) 8-microsatellite constellation, exploits forward-scattered bistatic L-band signals ($\lambda \approx 19\text{ cm}$, $1.575\text{ GHz}$) transmitted by civilian GPS constellations. Operating at microwave wavelengths, GNSS-R signals penetrate cloud cover and dense forest canopies with sub-daily revisit cadences ($< 7\text{ hours}$ median revisit) across tropical and subtropical latitudes ($38^\circ\text{S}$ to $38^\circ\text{N}$).

The fundamental physical measurement collected by spaceborne GNSS-R receivers is the two-dimensional \textbf{Delay-Doppler Map (DDM)}, representing the cross-correlation power of the reflected signal across code delay $\tau$ and Doppler frequency shift $f_D$:
\begin{equation}
    \left\langle |Y(\tau, f_D)|^2 \right\rangle = \frac{T_i^2 P_t G_t}{4\pi R_{ts}^2} \iint_{\mathcal{A}} \frac{G_r(\boldsymbol{\rho}) \sigma_0(\boldsymbol{\rho})}{4\pi R_{sr}^2(\boldsymbol{\rho})} \Lambda^2(\tau - \tau(\boldsymbol{\rho})) |S(f_D - f_D(\boldsymbol{\rho}))|^2 d\boldsymbol{\rho},
    \label{eq:bistatic_ddm}
\end{equation}
where $P_t G_t$ is the GPS transmitter effective isotropic radiated power, $G_r$ is the receiver antenna gain, $R_{ts}$ and $R_{sr}$ are geometric distances from transmitter/receiver to the specular surface reflection point $\boldsymbol{\rho}$, and $\Lambda(\cdot)$ and $S(\cdot)$ are the GPS PRN code cross-correlation and Doppler filter functions. The bistatic radar cross section $\sigma_0(\boldsymbol{\rho})$ is directly determined by the complex soil dielectric permittivity $\varepsilon = \varepsilon' + i\varepsilon''$ (which scales monotonically with volumetric soil moisture $\theta_v$), modulated by surface roughness $k\sigma_z$ and vegetation attenuation $e^{-2\tau_v}$.

Resolving the non-linear coupling between roughness, vegetation, and specular reflection, Nabi et al.~\cite{nabi2023quasiglobal} established the first \textbf{quasi-global assessment of deep learning-based CYGNSS soil moisture retrieval}. Rather than discarding spatial DDM structure through empirical 1D observable reduction (such as leading-edge slope or peak SNR), their convolutional neural network ingests the full 2D delay-Doppler cross-correlation power matrix combined with ancillary land surface parameters. Validated across the International Soil Moisture Network (ISMN) and SMAP radiometer grids, the deep learning model achieves an unbiased root-mean-square difference of $\text{ubRMSD} \approx 0.035\text{ m}^3/\text{m}^3$ and Pearson correlation $R = 0.89$ across diverse land-cover biomes, surpassing classical physical inversion algorithms.

Extending GNSS-R deep learning to emergency flood hydrology, Song et al.~\cite{song2022dualbranch} formulated a \textbf{Dual-Branch Neural Network (DBNN)} for all-weather flood inundation detection in South Asia. Their architecture combines: (1) a 2D convolutional branch that processes full CYGNSS delay-Doppler matrices to extract structural scattering signatures, and (2) a multi-layer perceptron branch ingesting auxiliary surface reflectivity and SMAP soil moisture time series. Across catastrophic monsoonal flood surges in the Ganges--Brahmaputra and Indus basins, DBNN achieves \textbf{91.8\%} flood detection accuracy ($F_1 = 0.884$), providing continuous cloud-penetrating inundation tracking and dynamic water routing where optical satellite constellations experienced complete cloud-mask obstruction.

\subsection{Oceanography and Marine Systems}
Ocean dynamics govern planetary heat distribution, carbon uptake, and marine biogeochemical cycles. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2024xihe} developed XiHe, a hierarchical ocean transformer trained on 28 years of GLORYS12V1 reanalysis at eddy-resolving $1/12^\circ$ resolution. By incorporating land-ocean boundary masking and multi-level vertical attention, XiHe models mesoscale eddies, sea surface temperature anomalies, and 3D velocity vectors, outperforming traditional operational hydrodynamic models (e.g., PSY4, GIOPS) while generating 10-day global forecasts in under 0.4 seconds. Addressing the extreme sparsity of oceanic observation networks, 4DVarNet~\cite{fablet2021learning} formulates neural variational data assimilation to invert along-track satellite altimetry into continuous 2D sea surface height (SSH) dynamics, demonstrating that learnable unrolled variational solvers reconstruct fine-scale mesoscale eddies and oceanic kinetic energy spectra far more effectively than classical optimal interpolation (OI).

To standardize evaluation across emerging marine foundation models, El Aouni et al.~\cite{elaouni2025oceanbench} established \textbf{OceanBench}, an open operational benchmarking suite for data-driven global ocean forecasting systems developed in collaboration with Mercator Ocean International. OceanBench benchmarks predictive skill for sea surface height (SSH), sea surface temperature (SST), and horizontal current velocities across 1- to 10-day lead times against CMEMS GLORYS12 reanalyses and in-situ altimetry observations. Bridging physical ocean simulations with cross-modal scientific reasoning, Bi et al.~\cite{bi2024oceangpt} introduced OceanGPT, the first domain-specific foundation language model for marine science. Pretrained and aligned on the DoInstruct corpus comprising multimodal oceanographic time series (temperature-salinity profiles from Argo float arrays and mooring buoys), sensor trajectories, and marine scientific literature, OceanGPT demonstrates autonomous reasoning across ocean dynamics, thermohaline circulation changes, and marine phenomenon attribution under physical consistency constraints.

\subsubsection{Wide-Swath Satellite Altimetry and Oceanic Internal Solitary Wave Inversion}
Conventional nadir satellite altimeters (e.g., Jason-3, Sentinel-3) measure sea surface height along 1D ground tracks separated by hundreds of kilometers, leaving submesoscale eddy dynamics and high-frequency internal waves unobserved. The launch of the Surface Water and Ocean Topography (\textbf{SWOT}) mission, featuring the Ka-band Radar Interferometer (\textbf{KaRIn}), marks a major transition by providing 2D swath measurements ($120\text{ km}$ total swath width) at fine resolutions down to $250\text{ m}$ to $2\text{ km}$. However, KaRIn interferometric measurements are corrupted by roll errors, baseline phase drift, and high-frequency instrument noise, which severely mask submesoscale coherent structures.

To address this inversion challenge without relying on scarce supervised in-situ targets, Cutolo et al.~\cite{cutolo2025swot} developed \textbf{SIMPGEN} (Simulation-Informed Metric and Prior for Generative Ensemble Networks). SIMPGEN formulates an unsupervised adversarial learning framework that conditions real SWOT swath observations on high-resolution numerical ocean simulations (NATL60). The architecture integrates a wavelet-informed multiscale neural loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}(\hat{\mathbf{h}}, \mathbf{h}_{\text{sim}}) + \lambda_{\text{wavelet}} \|\mathcal{W}\{\hat{\mathbf{h}}\} - \mathcal{W}\{\mathbf{h}_{\text{SWOT}}\}\|_1,
    \label{eq:simpgen_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{W}$ denotes a 2D discrete wavelet decomposition that isolates high-frequency noise from geostrophic and submesoscale baroclinic eddies. Applied to real SWOT swaths over the North Atlantic and Western Mediterranean, SIMPGEN effectively filters instrument noise while preserving coherent $15\text{--}50\text{ km}$ eddy structures and kinetic energy spectral slopes ($k^{-3}$ to $k^{-5/3}$), outperforming standard optimal interpolation and Gaussian smoothing.

Complementing surface topography filtering, Wan et al.~\cite{wan2024ste} tackled the detection and inversion of \textbf{Oceanic Internal Solitary Waves (ISWs)} using multimodal satellite remote sensing. Internal solitary waves propagate along interior pycnoclines, transporting massive kinetic energy that impacts offshore engineering, acoustic propagation, and submarine navigation. While optical sensors are frequently obscured by cloud cover, spaceborne altimetry and synthetic aperture radar (SAR) capture sea surface roughness modulated by internal wave divergence. However, altimetric ISW signatures suffer from extreme label sparsity and multiscale morphological variations.

Wan et al. proposed \textbf{STE-Net} (Scale-Translation Equivariant Network), explicitly embedding continuous scale-translation Lie symmetry into convolutional operator layers:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(T_{\mathbf{a}} S_s \mathbf{x}) = T_{\mathbf{a}} S_s \Phi(\mathbf{x}),
    \label{eq:ste_equivariance}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathbf{a}}$ denotes spatial translation by $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and $S_s$ denotes continuous isotropic scaling by factor $s > 0$. By enforcing geometric equivariance, STE-Net guarantees that localized wave packets are identified regardless of their spatial location or physical wavelength. Furthermore, the authors pretrain the network on massive unannotated global altimetry tracks using the SimCLR self-supervised contrastive framework. In benchmark evaluations on real oceanic altimetry and SAR datasets, STE-Net achieves an $F_1$ score of \textbf{0.884}, outperforming standard ResNet baselines ($F_1 = 0.762$) and demonstrating that physics-grounded Lie symmetries mitigate extreme data scarcity in spaceborne oceanographic monitoring.

\subsubsection{Satellite Scatterometry Constellations and Ocean Vector Wind Nowcasting}
Spaceborne microwave scatterometers (such as the European MetOp ASCAT series, the Chinese HY-2 satellite constellation, the Sino-French CFOSAT, and the Indian ScatSat/Oceansat) provide high-resolution measurements of ocean surface roughness by transmitting polarized radar pulses and measuring the normalized radar backscatter cross section $\sigma_0(\theta, \phi, \mathbf{u}_{10})$. The relationship between $\sigma_0$ and the 10-meter neutral ocean surface wind vector $\mathbf{u}_{10} = (u_{10}, \phi_w)$ is parameterized via empirical Geophysical Model Functions (GMF):
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_0 = f_{\text{GMF}}(u_{10}, \phi_w - \phi_a, \theta, p),
    \label{eq:gmf_formulation}
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ is radar incidence angle, $\phi_a$ is radar azimuth, $\phi_w$ is wind direction, and $p$ denotes radar polarization ($VV$ or $HH$). While scatterometer observations are routinely assimilated into global numerical weather prediction (NWP) systems, operational NWP data assimilation cycles require 3-to-6-hour latency windows and global spectral filtering, failing to exploit the immediate observational fidelity of satellite passes for short-term (intraday) nowcasting.

Pinto et al.~\cite{pinto2026windcastnet} introduced \textbf{WindCastNet}, the first deep learning nowcasting architecture directly predicting future offshore wind speed and direction fields from raw satellite scatterometer constellations. Scatterometer observations present severe spatio-temporal irregularities: individual satellite ground swaths cover narrow strips ($\sim 500\text{--}1000\text{ km}$), sampling orbits are asynchronous, and local revisit intervals vary from hours to days. WindCastNet resolves these irregularities using a \textbf{Partial Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory (PConv-LSTM)} network:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{W}' = \mathbf{W} \odot \frac{\mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{M} + \epsilon}, \quad \mathbf{M}' = f(\mathbf{M}),
    \label{eq:pconv_masking}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{M} \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$ is a binary spatial observation mask dynamically tracking unobserved oceanic pixels, and continuous inter-observation intervals $\Delta t$ are encoded via sinusoidal temporal embeddings. Evaluated over the North Sea offshore wind energy corridor, WindCastNet reduces root-mean-square error by \textbf{23\% at 1 hour} and \textbf{7\% at 2 hours} relative to the operational numerical HARMONIE MEPS model, while outperforming persistence baselines by 9--15\% across the first three forecast hours.

Addressing multi-sensor cross-calibration and tropical cyclone gale retrieval, Xiang et al.~\cite{xiang2024scatterometer} demonstrated an active-passive satellite fusion architecture combining spaceborne scatterometer backscatter $\sigma_0$ with multi-frequency passive microwave radiometer brightness temperatures (TB). Under severe rain squalls and high wind conditions ($u_{10} > 20\text{ m/s}$), scatterometer radar signals suffer from atmospheric attenuation and rain volume scattering, creating wind ambiguity. By jointly processing active scatterometer backscatter and passive radiometer channels via deep convolutional networks, Xiang et al. achieved a root-mean-square deviation of \textbf{$0.7\text{ m/s}$} against ASCAT and \textbf{$1.1\text{ m/s}$} against in-situ moored buoys, demonstrating robust wind speed recovery across oceanic fronts and cyclone tracks.

\subsection{Geophysics and Seismology}
In seismology, continuous 3-component acoustic waveforms record tectonic stress accumulation and seismic rupture. While classical phase-picking baselines like PhaseNet~\cite{zhu2019phasenet} utilized supervised 1D U-Nets for local seismic windows, modern foundational architectures such as SeisT~\cite{li2024seist} train hierarchical seismogram transformers on tens of millions of global waveforms. SeisT supports multi-task downstream adaptation, simultaneously performing earthquake detection, high-precision P/S phase picking, initial-motion polarity determination, and magnitude/epicenter regression. Providing the standardized foundational infrastructure for seismic machine learning, Woollam et al.~\cite{woollam2022seisbench} released \textbf{SeisBench}, unifying 30+ million waveforms across global catalogues (ETHZ, GEOFON, INSTANCE, SCEDC, STEAD) into a standardized multi-task evaluation and transfer benchmark.

Advancing beyond discrete phase picking to continuous wavefield modeling and subsurface tomography, Yang et al.~\cite{yang2021seismic} formulated \textbf{WaveFNO}, establishing the first continuous Fourier neural operator framework for seismic wave propagation and full-waveform inversion (FWI). Solving the 2D/3D elastodynamic wave equation across heterogeneous, discontinuous velocity models, WaveFNO maps acoustic and elastic impedance directly to continuous displacement wavefields $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t)$. Because the neural operator is end-to-end differentiable, full-waveform velocity updates $\nabla_c \mathcal{J}$ are obtained via reverse-mode automatic differentiation in seconds, outperforming conventional finite-difference wave simulators by several orders of magnitude while circumventing cycle-skipping local minima in subsurface imaging.

\subsection{Polar Cryosphere and Sea Ice Dynamics}
\label{sec:polar_cryosphere}
Polar cryospheric dynamics---governing Arctic and Antarctic sea ice extent, ice-sheet mass balance, and permafrost degradation---exert profound thermodynamic control over planetary albedo and global thermohaline circulation. However, polar regions suffer from acute geographic observation gaps, severe seasonal cloud cover obscuring optical satellites, and complex non-linear rheology governing sea ice fracture, drift, and melt-pond formation.

Addressing long-range seasonal sea ice forecasting, Andersson et al.~\cite{andersson2021icenet} established \textbf{IceNet}, a probabilistic deep learning system predicting monthly Arctic sea ice concentration maps up to 6 months in advance. Trained on 2,192 years of CMIP6 climate model simulations and calibrated against 40 years of observational sea ice concentration data from OSI-SAF and ERA5 reanalyses, IceNet employs an ensemble of U-Net architectures with temperature scaling to produce calibrated probabilistic forecasts of sea ice presence (concentration $>15\%$). Operating at 25-km spatial resolution, IceNet demonstrably surpasses the operational ECMWF SEAS5 dynamical seasonal physics model, particularly in predicting anomalous summer ice retreat and extreme September minimum sea ice extents.

To address the lack of standardized machine learning evaluation across polar remote sensing, Taleghan et al.~\cite{taleghan2025icebench} introduced \textbf{IceBench}, a comprehensive multi-modal benchmark for deep learning-based sea ice type classification. Built upon the AI4Arctic Sea Ice Challenge corpus, IceBench unifies dual-polarization Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imagery, AMSR2 microwave radiometer brightness temperatures, and ERA5 atmospheric forcings across operational ice chart labels from the Danish Meteorological Institute (DMI). By standardizing evaluation protocols across pixel-based (U-Net, SegNet) and patch-based (Vision Transformer, ResNet) architectures, IceBench provides the first rigorous benchmark assessing spatial transferability across distinct Arctic sub-regions and temporal transferability across transition seasons (freeze-up versus summer melt).

\subsubsection{Viscoelastic Ice-Sheet Rheology, Deep Operator Grounding Line Migration, and Automated Calving Front Extraction}
Projections of future global sea-level rise are dominated by deep uncertainties surrounding the dynamic retreat of polar ice sheets in Antarctica and Greenland. Ice-sheet flow is governed by non-Newtonian viscous creeping flow described by Glen's flow law:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} = A \tau_e^{n-1} \boldsymbol{\tau}_{(d)},
    \label{eq:glens_law}
\end{equation}
where $\dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} = \frac{1}{2}(\nabla \mathbf{u} + (\nabla \mathbf{u})^T)$ is the strain-rate tensor, $\boldsymbol{\tau}_{(d)}$ is the deviatoric stress tensor, $\tau_e = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} \text{tr}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{(d)}^2)}$ is effective stress, $A(T)$ is temperature-dependent ice softness, and the rheological exponent is typically $n \approx 3$. In depth-integrated ice-stream and ice-shelf domains, horizontal ice velocity $\mathbf{u} = (u, v)$ is modeled via the 2D Shallow-Shelf Approximation (SSA) non-linear elliptic boundary value problem:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \cdot \left( 2 \bar{\mu} H \left( \nabla \mathbf{u} + \nabla \mathbf{u}^T + 2(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u})\mathbf{I} \right) \right) - \beta^2 \mathbf{u} = \rho_i g H \nabla s,
    \label{eq:ssa_equation}
\end{equation}
where $H$ is ice thickness, $s$ is surface elevation, $\rho_i$ is ice density, $\beta^2(\mathbf{x})$ is a basal friction coefficient, and $\bar{\mu}$ is depth-averaged effective viscosity depending non-linearly on velocity gradients $\nabla \mathbf{u}$. Solving Eq.~(\ref{eq:ssa_equation}) within forward climate ensembles requires thousands of non-linear Picard or Newton--Raphson iterations, creating a severe computational barrier for probabilistic risk assessment.

He et al.~\cite{he2023hybrid} resolved this bottleneck by formulating a \textbf{hybrid Deep Operator Network / Finite Element Method (DeepONet-FEM)} for ice-sheet modeling. DeepONet emulates the non-linear operator mapping spatial fields of ice thickness $H(\mathbf{x})$, bed topography $b(\mathbf{x})$, and basal slipperiness $\beta(\mathbf{x})$ directly to the diagnostic horizontal velocity field $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^P b_k(H, b, \beta) \, t_k(\mathbf{x}),
    \label{eq:deeponet_ice}
\end{equation}
where $b_k$ are branch network outputs and $t_k$ are trunk basis functions. Crucially, the diagnostic neural operator predictions are coupled directly to a finite element Galerkin solver for transient ice thickness advection $\partial_t H + \nabla \cdot (H \mathbf{u}) = \dot{a}$. On the benchmark Marine Ice Sheet Model Intercomparison Project (MISMIP+) and synthetic Antarctic ice-stream geometries, the hybrid operator accelerates forward velocity evaluations by \textbf{over $1{,}000\times$} while maintaining relative $L_2$ errors under $1.5\%$ and strictly preserving finite-element mass conservation across multi-century grounding line retreat simulations.

Complementing interior ice rheology, the catastrophic disintegration of marine-terminating outlet glaciers is governed by iceberg calving at the marine terminus. Daniel Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2021calfin} established the \textbf{Calving Front Machine (CALFIN)}, an automated deep learning framework for generating multi-decadal calving front time series from multi-satellite Earth observation. Using an optimized DeepLabv3+ convolutional neural network with a modified Xception backbone, CALFIN processes heterogeneous Synthetic Aperture Radar (Sentinel-1, TerraSAR-X) and optical (Landsat 1--8) satellite scenes spanning \textbf{47 years (1972--2019)} across 66 Greenland outlet glaciers. By integrating automated coastal boundary masking and sub-pixel distance transforms, CALFIN extracted over \textbf{22,678 individual calving front positions} with a mean localization error of \textbf{$86.4\text{ m}$}, outperforming manual human delineation under severe polar night, dense cloud obscuration, and sea-ice mélange conditions. CALFIN provides the empirical boundary data essential to calibrate neural operator calving rate parameterizations in planetary sea-level projections.

\subsection{Geo-Energy, Geothermal Reservoirs, and Subsurface Carbon Sequestration}
\label{sec:geo_energy}
The transition to zero-carbon energy systems fundamentally depends on deep subsurface reservoirs for geological carbon sequestration (GCS), geothermal energy extraction, and underground hydrogen storage. Subsurface fluid dynamics are governed by non-linear, multi-phase Darcy-Brinkman flow through highly heterogeneous, anisotropic geological formations:
\begin{equation}
    \phi(\mathbf{x}) \frac{\partial (\rho_\alpha S_\alpha)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho_\alpha \mathbf{u}_\alpha) = q_\alpha, \quad \mathbf{u}_\alpha = -\frac{k_{r\alpha}(S_\alpha) \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x})}{\mu_\alpha} \left( \nabla p_\alpha - \rho_\alpha \mathbf{g} \right),
    \label{eq:multiphase_darcy}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \in \{\text{CO}_2, \text{brine}\}$ denotes fluid phases, $\phi(\mathbf{x})$ is formation porosity, $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x})$ is the heterogeneous permeability tensor, $S_\alpha$ is phase saturation ($S_{\text{CO}_2} + S_{\text{brine}} = 1$), $k_{r\alpha}$ is relative permeability, $\mu_\alpha$ is dynamic viscosity, and $p_\alpha$ represents phase pressure. Because traditional numerical reservoir simulators (such as CMG GEM, TOUGH2, or Eclipse) require hours to days per run to solve coupled non-linear pressure-saturation equations on fine 3D computational grids, foundation neural operator models have emerged as high-leverage surrogates for real-time reservoir monitoring, risk assessment, and closed-loop well control.

\subsubsection{U-FNO: Enhanced Multiscale Fourier Neural Operators for Multiphase Flow}
Addressing the limitations of standard Fourier neural operators on discontinuous fronts, Wen et al.~\cite{wen2022ufno} pioneered the \textbf{U-FNO} (U-Net enhanced Fourier Neural Operator). Standard FNO applies global frequency-domain truncations that exhibit Gibbs oscillations and oversmooth sharp saturation shocks between injected supercritical $\text{CO}_2$ and ambient saline water. U-FNO resolves this by interleaving global 2D/3D Fourier spectral convolution layers with multiscale U-Net spatial skip connections:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}_{l+1}(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma\left( \mathcal{W}_l * \mathbf{v}_l(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{W}_{\text{spatial}}^{(l)} \mathbf{v}_l(\mathbf{x}) + \mathcal{U}_{\text{skip}}^{(l)}(\mathbf{v}_l)(\mathbf{x}) \right).
    \label{eq:ufno_layer}
\end{equation}
Trained on extensive multiphase GCS simulation suites under realistic permeability fields with strong spatial correlation lengths, U-FNO accurately forecasts 30-year multi-phase $\text{CO}_2$ gas-phase saturation fronts and pressure buildup maps. U-FNO achieves over a \textbf{$10{,}000\times$ speedup} relative to numerical PDE solvers while reducing gas saturation prediction errors by over $50\%$ compared to standard convolutional architectures, enabling real-time risk evaluation of caprock fracture and induced seismicity.

\subsubsection{CCSNet: Full-Physics Deep Learning Modeling Suite with Real Thermodynamic EOS}
Scaling operator modeling to industrial carbon capture and storage operations, Wen, Hay, and Benson~\cite{wen2021ccsnet} established \textbf{CCSNet}. Industrial GCS involves complex thermodynamic phase behavior across wide ranges of reservoir temperatures ($30^\circ\text{C}\text{--}120^\circ\text{C}$), pressures ($10\text{--}40\text{ MPa}$), and salinities ($0\text{--}250{,}000\text{ ppm}$). CCSNet couples multi-scale neural operators directly to a data-driven thermodynamic Equation of State (EOS) for supercritical $\text{CO}_2$ and brine:
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\text{CO}_2} = f_{\text{EOS}}(p, T), \quad \mu_{\text{CO}_2} = g_{\text{EOS}}(p, T, \rho_{\text{CO}_2}),
\end{equation}
guaranteeing thermodynamic consistency across sub-critical and supercritical fluid regimes. Pretrained on over $20{,}000$ synthetic heterogeneous reservoir simulations across variable injection rates, well configurations, and geological facies, CCSNet evaluates 50-year structural, capillary, and solubility trapping in milliseconds. This empowers field operators to perform real-time optimization of well placement and dynamically steer injection rates to maximize long-term storage security.

\subsubsection{Geo-FNO: Multiphase Flow on Complex and Faulted Geometries}
Complementing these models, Li et al.~\cite{li2022geofno} demonstrated \textbf{Geo-FNO} on 3D subsurface flow and geomechanical deformation. Natural reservoir strata are rarely simple Cartesian boxes; they contain inclined layers, structural faults, and pinch-outs. By learning smooth coordinate transformations $\boldsymbol{\phi}: \Omega \to [0, 1]^d$, Geo-FNO maps complex faulted geological geometries into canonical reference grids, executing continuous operator inference without losing boundary fidelity or requiring costly unstructured mesh generation.

\subsubsection{Multi-Input and Nested Fourier Operators for 3D Decadal Geological Carbon Sequestration: Fourier-MIONet and Nested Fourier-DeepONet}
Industrial geological carbon sequestration requires modeling supercritical $\text{CO}_2$ plume migration and pressure evolution over multi-decadal horizons ($30\text{--}50\text{ years}$) in response to variable engineering operations. This poses two fundamental operator learning challenges: (1) \textit{Heterogeneous Functional Inputs}: simulations depend simultaneously on static, high-dimensional spatial fields (heterogeneous 3D permeability $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x})$, porosity $\phi(\mathbf{x})$, and facies boundaries) and time-dependent continuous control trajectories (well injection schedules $q_{\text{inj}}(t)$, shut-in cycles, and dynamic bottom-hole pressure constraints); and (2) \textit{3D Volumetric Curse of Dimensionality}: evaluating multi-scale multiphase Darcy flow on full 3D reservoir grids ($N_x \times N_y \times N_z \times T$) leads to prohibitive GPU memory footprints.

To solve the multi-input functional coupling, Zhongyi Jiang, Min Zhu, and Lu Lu~\cite{jiang2024fouriermionet} pioneered \textbf{Fourier-MIONet} (\textit{Fourier-enhanced Multiple-Input Neural Operator}). Rather than forcing heterogeneous inputs into a single convolutional or spectral tensor, Fourier-MIONet formulates a dedicated multi-branch architecture:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G}\left(\mathbf{K}, \phi, q_{\text{inj}}\right)(\mathbf{x}, t) = \sum_{k=1}^P \left[ \mathcal{B}_1^{(k)}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\text{FNO}}(\mathbf{K}, \phi)\right) \odot \mathcal{B}_2^{(k)}\left(q_{\text{inj}}\right) \right] \mathcal{T}^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}, t),
    \label{eq:fourier_mionet}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}_{\text{FNO}}$ is a Fourier neural operator feature extractor learning spatial representations of heterogeneous rock properties, $\mathcal{B}_2$ is a dynamic recurrent branch encoding temporal injection rates, and $\mathcal{T}$ represents trunk coordinate basis networks evaluated at spatio-temporal query points $(\mathbf{x}, t)$. Evaluated on heterogeneous saline aquifer benchmarks (SPE10 and realistic fluvial reservoir formations), Fourier-MIONet simultaneously predicts the transient 30-year evolution of supercritical $\text{CO}_2$ gas saturation $S_g(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and dynamic pressure buildup $\Delta P(\mathbf{x}, t)$ with mean relative errors under \textbf{2.8\%}. Crucially, Fourier-MIONet achieves an inference acceleration exceeding \textbf{$10{,}000\times$} relative to commercial compositional finite-volume simulators (e.g., CMG-GEM or Schlumberger Eclipse), enabling comprehensive Monte Carlo caprock integrity assessment and leakage risk quantification across thousands of geological realization ensembles.

Addressing full 3D volumetric scaling under multi-well injection, Jonathan E. Lee et al.~\cite{lee2024nested} developed the \textbf{Nested Fourier-DeepONet} framework. Lee et al. decompose the high-dimensional 3D reservoir operator into a hierarchical nested structure: an outer 3D Fourier operator models non-local spatial fluid advection across stratified reservoir layers, while an inner temporal-pressure operator network resolves steep pressure gradients in the near-wellbore vicinity. Evaluated across complex 3D geological reservoirs with non-stationary permeability correlation lengths, Nested Fourier-DeepONet bounds relative $L_2$ errors in 3D saturation and pressure to under \textbf{2.5\%}, demonstrating high generalizability to arbitrary well locations and shut-in regimes while reducing training memory overhead by over $65\%$ compared to monolithic 3D neural operators.



\subsection{Space Weather and Heliophysics}
Space weather phenomena---including solar flares, coronal mass ejections (CMEs), and geomagnetic storms---exert severe risks on satellite infrastructure, GPS navigation, and terrestrial power grids. Roy et al.~\cite{roy2025surya} introduced Surya, a 366-million parameter foundation model for heliophysics trained on 13 years of continuous multi-wavelength extreme ultraviolet imagery from NASA's Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO). Surya incorporates spectral gating and long-short attention to track active region magnetic helicity, delivering state-of-the-art solar flare and solar wind forecasting.

Advancing from empirical image tracking to continuous physical field solvers, Du et al.~\cite{du2024glfno} established the \textbf{Global-Local Fourier Neural Operator (GL-FNO)} for coronal magnetic field simulation. By coupling a global low-frequency spectral branch with a high-resolution local active-region branch, GL-FNO satisfies the magnetic solenoidal constraint $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$ while accelerating 3D MHD simulations by over \textbf{$20{,}000\times$} ($0.04\text{ s}$ per state). Extending neural operators throughout the heliosphere, Mansouri et al.~\cite{mansouri2025solarwind} introduced \textbf{Solar Wind SFNO}, an operational spherical harmonic operator surrogate that models continuous radial solar wind plasma velocity from $30 R_\odot$ to 1 AU. Operating at 20 milliseconds per forecast, Solar Wind SFNO accurately captures non-linear stream interaction regions and shock arrivals, closing the computational loop from solar surface activity to geomagnetic storm defense on Earth.

\subsection{Cross-Disciplinary Universal Time Series Foundations}
Bridging physical boundaries between disparate earth science subdomains, universal foundation models like UniTS~\cite{gao2024units} and MOMENT~\cite{goswami2024moment} demonstrate that pretraining on multi-domain scientific collections enables cross-physics zero-shot transfer. By decoupling sequence tokens from variable identities and applying prompt-conditioned task masks, universal backbones can be deployed across river streamflow, power grids, seismic sensors, and atmospheric stations without re-training architectural weights.
