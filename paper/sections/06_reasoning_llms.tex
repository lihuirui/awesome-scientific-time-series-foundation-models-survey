\section{Scientific Reasoning LLMs and Autonomous Agents}
\label{sec:reasoning_llms}

\subsection{The Paradigm Shift: From Numerical Fitting to Scientific Reasoning}
While foundation backbones excel at numerical continuous pattern matching, complex scientific discovery necessitates logical reasoning, physical hypothesis formulation, and contextual interpretation. Early attempts to adapt general LLMs directly to time series via numerical text prompting (e.g., feeding raw floating-point numbers as text tokens) incurred severe limitations: token sequence inflation, numerical precision truncation, and inability to capture continuous derivatives~\cite{jin2024whatcan}. Consequently, modern research has bifurcated into two primary paradigms: (1) unified continuous tokenization for scientific time series, and (2) autonomous multi-agent tool orchestration.

\subsection{Unified Continuous Tokenization: SciTS and TimeOmni}
Wu et al.~\cite{wu2025scits} introduced \textbf{SciTS}, the first large-scale cross-disciplinary benchmark designed specifically to evaluate LLMs on scientific time series understanding and generation. Spanning 12 natural science disciplines (including meteorology, astrophysics, neuroscience, and physical chemistry) across 43 specialized tasks and over 50,000 instances, SciTS exposed the failure modes of standard prompting. To resolve these deficiencies, the authors proposed \textbf{TimeOmni}, a unified architecture featuring a multi-scale continuous patch tokenizer that projects 1D and multi-dimensional scientific series into dense semantic embeddings aligned with pre-trained LLM latent spaces. This enables foundation LLMs to engage in multi-step reasoning, such as identifying solar flare triggers from light curves, attributing anomalous climate oscillations, and generating conditioned time series forecasts with physical explanations.

\subsection{Frequency-Aware Contextual Integration: ClimateLLM}
Rather than treating atmospheric series as unstructured text, Li et al.~\cite{li2025climatellm} introduced \textbf{ClimateLLM}. This framework decomposes multi-station and gridded meteorological sequences into orthogonal frequency bands via Fourier transform modules. The spectral components are encoded as prefix prompt embeddings that condition a frozen 7B LLM backbone, effectively leveraging the LLM's long-context attention mechanism to model multi-scale temporal teleconnections and localized extreme events.

\subsection{Domain-Specialized Foundation LLMs: K2 for Geoscience}
Adapting general-purpose LLMs to specialized Earth sciences necessitates injection of structured geological and environmental knowledge. Deng et al.~\cite{deng2024k2} introduced \textbf{K2}, a foundational language model tailored for geoscience knowledge understanding and utilization. Pretrained on over 5.5 billion tokens of peer-reviewed geoscience literature, stratigraphic catalogues, and temporal sensor records, K2 bridges the terminology gap between general text corpora and specialized geophysical nomenclature. Through the \textit{GeoSignal} instruction-tuning protocol, K2 learns to reason across temporal geological processes, infer lithological fault dynamics, and assist researchers in interpreting complex spatio-temporal sensor logs with physical groundedness.

\subsection{Multimodal Vision-Language Grounding: GeoChat}
Satellite and aerial Earth observation series frequently require aligning visual changes with natural language queries. Kuckreja et al.~\cite{kuckreja2024geochat} developed \textbf{GeoChat}, the first grounded large vision-language model explicitly engineered for remote sensing. Built upon a Vicuna-1.5 language backbone coupled with a high-resolution CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder via LoRA adaptation, GeoChat processes multi-spectral satellite imagery and temporal revisit pairs. Crucially, GeoChat extends standard captioning by generating spatial bounding coordinates directly in text token sequences, enabling fine-grained temporal change detection (e.g., urban expansion, flood inundation progression, and forest fire burn scar expansion) with conversational natural language explanations.

\subsection{Reprogramming Pretrained Language Models: Time-LLM}
Rather than training domain transformers from scratch or fine-tuning entire parameter spaces, Jin et al.~\cite{jin2024timellm} introduced \textbf{Time-LLM}, pioneering the paradigm of reprogramming frozen large language models for time series forecasting. Time-LLM recognizes that pre-trained language backbones (such as LLaMA-7B or GPT-2) possess exceptional cross-modal pattern recognition and sequence abstraction capabilities. To align modalities, Time-LLM:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Input Patch Reprogramming}: Divides continuous input series into patches and projects them into the word token embedding space through a linear reprogramming layer conditioned on cross-attention with textual domain prototypes.
    \item \textbf{Prompt-as-Prefix Conditioning}: Augments the reprogrammed patches with domain task instructions (e.g., dataset semantics, sampling frequency, and trend descriptions) as natural language prefix prompts.
    \item \textbf{Frozen Backbone Inference}: Keeps the foundational LLM weights entirely frozen during training, tuning only lightweight linear adapters.
\end{enumerate}
By reprogramming frozen language models, Time-LLM achieves superior few-shot and zero-shot performance across scientific and benchmark datasets, proving that language model representations serve as universal priors for temporal dynamics.

\subsection{Autonomous Multi-Agent Climate Workflows: ClimateAgent}
Moving beyond single-model inference, Li et al.~\cite{li2025climateagent} introduced \textbf{ClimateAgent}, an autonomous multi-agent orchestration framework for complex climate data science. Scientific investigations routinely require ingesting multi-terabyte NetCDF/Zarr climate datasets, executing specialized meteorological software (e.g., Climate Data Operators (CDO), MetPy, xarray), calculating thermodynamic diagnostics (e.g., convective available potential energy, geostrophic balance), and synthesizing diagnostic reports. ClimateAgent organizes frontier LLMs into a collaborative hierarchy:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Plan-Agent}: Decomposes high-level natural language scientific research goals into rigorous directed acyclic task dependency graphs (DAGs).
    \item \textbf{Data-Agent}: Navigates distributed scientific data archives (Copernicus Climate Data Store, NASA Earthdata) to retrieve reanalysis and satellite products, managing chunking and caching.
    \item \textbf{Coding-Agent}: Synthesizes and executes Python/Bash diagnostic code in secure sandboxed environments with automated error recovery loops, iteratively inspecting stack traces to debug array shape and temporal coordinate alignment errors.
    \item \textbf{Critic-Agent}: Verifies physical plausibility, evaluating dimensional unit consistency and testing for energy conservation bounds before assembling peer-review-ready figures and reports.
\end{itemize}
Evaluated on the standardized \textit{Climate-Agent-Bench-85}, ClimateAgent demonstrated an 82\% task completion rate without human intervention, marking a concrete transition from static numerical forecasters to self-directed AI co-scientists capable of hypothesis formulation and autonomous verification in natural Earth systems.

\subsection{Tool Invocation Protocols and Execution Sandboxes for Scientific Co-Scientists}
\label{sec:agent_tool_interfaces}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:agent_reasoning_dag}, the operational viability of autonomous scientific co-scientists hinges upon rigorous DAG scheduling, stateful computational sandboxes, dynamic error recovery loops, and physical plausibility guardrails:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical DAG Task Scheduling}: When presented with a complex scientific inquiry (e.g., assessing the dynamic intensification of an atmospheric river or Mediterranean marine heatwave), the Plan-Agent formulates a topological Directed Acyclic Graph. Nodes represent modular atomic subtasks (data query, regridding, thermodynamic flux computation, kinetic energy budget integration), while directed edges enforce data dependencies.
    \item \textbf{Structured Tool Invocation \& Distributed Ingestion}: Scientific foundation agents communicate with computational tools via structured JSON schema function calls. Rather than generating unconstrained code, the Data-Agent triggers domain-specific API endpoints (e.g., Copernicus Marine Toolbox, ECMWF CDS API, USGS Hydro-Data) and manages out-of-core sharding through \texttt{xarray} and \texttt{dask}, preventing memory exhaustion when handling multi-terabyte spatio-temporal datasets.
    \item \textbf{Stateful Computational Sandboxes}: Executing scientific workflows requires isolated REPL (Read-Eval-Print Loop) environments equipped with verified domain libraries:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Atmospheric \& Earth Observation}: \texttt{xarray} and \texttt{dask} for out-of-core chunked tensor operations; \texttt{MetPy} for thermodynamic diagrams and potential vorticity inversions; \texttt{rasterio} and \texttt{rioxarray} for satellite raster projections.
        \item \textit{Ocean Dynamics}: \texttt{oceanbench} and \texttt{copernicusmarine} client APIs for automated GLORYS and altimetry verification.
        \item \textit{Geophysics}: \texttt{ObsPy} and \texttt{SeisBench}~\cite{woollam2022seisbench} for streaming waveform filtering, phase picking, and seismic moment tensor inversions.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Traceback Self-Correction Loop}: Scientific data formats frequently exhibit idiosyncratic edge cases: inverted latitude coordinate ordering ($90^\circ \to -90^\circ$ vs. $-90^\circ \to 90^\circ$), non-standard calendars (e.g., 360-day climate model years in CMIP6), or incompatible vertical pressure unit conventions ($\text{Pa}$ vs. $\text{hPa}$). As depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:agent_reasoning_dag} (Loop 4), when the Python runtime emits an exception or dimension mismatch, the agent automatically captures the execution traceback, inspects array metadata (via \texttt{ds.dims} and \texttt{ds.coords}), and performs self-reflection to iteratively synthesize corrected code blocks.
    \item \textbf{Physical Plausibility Verification Guardrails}: Even syntactically error-free code may produce unphysical scientific outputs. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:agent_reasoning_dag} (Stage 5), the Critic-Agent acts as a domain-specific gatekeeper: evaluating strict conservation of mass and energy, checking non-negativity of physical quantities (precipitation $P \ge 0$, solar radiation $R \ge 0$), verifying bounded relative humidity ($0\% \le \text{RH} \le 100\%$), and enforcing dimensional consistency across derived units before approving publication-grade artifacts.
\end{enumerate}
