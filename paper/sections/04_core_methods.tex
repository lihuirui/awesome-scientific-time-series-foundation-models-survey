\section{Core Foundation Model Architectures}
\label{sec:core_methods}

\subsection{3D Spatial and Spherical Transformers}
Traditional 2D Vision Transformers process planar images by dividing them into uniform patches. However, planetary atmospheric states are inherently three-dimensional, structured across latitude, longitude, and geopotential pressure altitudes.

\subsubsection{Pangu-Weather: 3D Earth-Specific Transformer}
Bi et al.~\cite{bi2023pangu} introduced Pangu-Weather, the first deep learning weather forecasting system to demonstrably surpass top-tier operational NWP models (ECMWF IFS) in medium-range deterministic forecasting accuracy. Pangu-Weather formulates atmospheric prediction using a 3D Earth-Specific Transformer (3DEST) architecture. The state space is divided into 3D cuboid patches of size $2 \times 4 \times 4$ across vertical levels, latitude, and longitude. To account for inhomogeneous spatial dynamics (e.g., Coriolis force variations across latitudes and atmospheric lapse rates across altitudes), 3DEST replaces standard relative position biases with an Earth-Specific Positional Bias (ESPB) matrix indexed explicitly by pressure level and latitude:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d}} + B_{\text{Earth}}(l_i, l_j, \phi_i, \phi_j)\right)V.
\end{equation}
To counteract rapid error accumulation during iterative autoregressive rollout, Pangu-Weather trains four distinct models with lead times of 1, 3, 6, and 24 hours. A greedy temporal aggregation algorithm chains these models to generate 7-day global forecasts in seconds.

\subsubsection{FuXi: Cascade Forecasting System}
Recognizing that predictive uncertainty compounds non-linearly with lead time, Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023fuxi} developed FuXi, a cascade machine learning system consisting of three specialized Swin Transformer stages: FuXi-Short (0--42 hours), FuXi-Medium (42--120 hours), and FuXi-Long (120--360 hours). By dynamically switching between model weights optimized for different forecasting horizons, FuXi significantly curtails the blurring and spectral dissipation common in long-horizon neural forecasts.

\subsubsection{Aurora: Scaling to Planetary Foundation Models}
Bodnar et al.~\cite{bodnar2024aurora} demonstrated that scaling principles hold in Earth system modeling by introducing Aurora, a 1.3-billion parameter foundation model. Pretrained on over 1 petabyte of heterogeneous atmospheric data---including ERA5, ECMWF HRES, operational IFS analyses, and NOAA GFS---Aurora incorporates a 3D Perceiver architecture capable of accepting arbitrary combinations of vertical levels and surface variables at $0.1^\circ$ operational resolution ($10\text{ km}$ equivalent).

\subsubsection{AIFS: ECMWF Operational GNN-Transformer Architecture}
Lang et al.~\cite{lang2024aifs} developed the Artificial Intelligence Forecasting System (\textbf{AIFS}), ECMWF's official operational data-driven model. AIFS marries the geometric flexibility of graph neural networks with the long-range receptive fields of sliding-window transformers. The architecture maps icosahedral multi-mesh node representations through a graph encoder, processes latent multi-scale interactions via a local sliding-window transformer that limits attention memory to $O(N \cdot W)$ tokens, and decodes back to the operational reduced Gaussian grid via a graph decoder. Pretrained on decades of ERA5 reanalysis and fine-tuned on ECMWF's operational NWP analyses, AIFS runs in operational production at ECMWF four times daily alongside physics-based NWP systems.

\subsection{Multi-Mesh Spherical Graph Neural Networks}

\subsubsection{GraphCast: Planetary Multi-Mesh Routing}
Lam et al.~\cite{lam2023graphcast} pioneered an alternative geometric approach by formulating global weather forecasting over a multi-resolution spherical graph. Standard equirectangular projections concentrate excessive grid cells near the poles while diluting them at the equator. GraphCast circumvents this singularity using an Encode-Process-Decode framework operating on a hierarchical icosahedral mesh:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Grid-to-Mesh Encoder}: Maps 0.25$^\circ$ latitude-longitude grid variables onto the vertices of a 6-level refined icosahedral mesh $M_6$ ($40{,}962$ nodes, $122{,}880$ spherical edges).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Mesh Processor}: Employs 16 layers of homogeneous message passing across all hierarchical mesh levels ($M_0$ to $M_6$). Long-range edges in coarse mesh levels ($M_0, M_1$) facilitate rapid, global information propagation across planetary teleconnection pathways in few message-passing hops.
    \item \textbf{Mesh-to-Grid Decoder}: Projects refined mesh vertex features back to the 0.25$^\circ$ target grid to predict single-step 6-hour temporal increments $\Delta \mathcal{X}_t$.
\end{enumerate}
GraphCast is trained with a 12-step autoregressive loss rollout, enabling robust, physically stable 10-day medium-range forecasts that outperform ECMWF HRES on over 90\% of headline verification variables.

\subsection{Continuous Neural Operators}

\subsubsection{FourCastNet: Adaptive Fourier Neural Operators}
Pathak et al.~\cite{pathak2022fourcastnet} deployed Adaptive Fourier Neural Operators (AFNO) to solve planetary-scale atmospheric PDEs. Conventional spatial convolutions are intrinsically tied to fixed grid resolutions. In contrast, neural operators approximate non-linear mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces. AFNO transforms tokenized feature representations into the frequency domain via 2D Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT), applies block-diagonal weight multiplications with soft-thresholding sparsity in the frequency domain, and returns to physical space via inverse FFT:
\begin{equation}
    z_{\text{out}}(x) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\left( \text{SoftThreshold}\left(\mathbf{W} \cdot \mathcal{F}(z_{\text{in}})(k) \right) \right) + \mathbf{W}_s z_{\text{in}}(x).
\end{equation}
This formulation provides computational complexity quasi-linear in spatial resolution while demonstrating remarkable zero-shot super-resolution and transferability.

\subsubsection{SFNO: Spherical Fourier Neural Operators}
While planar Fourier transforms accelerate spectral computation, standard 2D FFTs applied to equirectangular latitude-longitude coordinates suffer from severe metric distortions near the poles, where meridians converge. Bonev et al.~\cite{bonev2023sfno} resolved this geometric bottleneck by formulating the Spherical Fourier Neural Operator (SFNO), which solves continuous PDEs directly on the Riemannian manifold of the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^2 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$.

Let colatitude be $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ and longitude be $\lambda \in [0, 2\pi)$. Any square-integrable scalar atmospheric state $u \in \mathcal{L}^2(\mathbb{S}^2)$ admits an orthogonal expansion in terms of spherical harmonics $Y_\ell^m(\theta, \lambda)$:
\begin{equation}
    u(\theta, \lambda) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{L} \sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell} \hat{u}_\ell^m Y_\ell^m(\theta, \lambda),
\end{equation}
where $\ell \in \mathbb{N}_0$ denotes the spherical degree, $m \in \{-\ell, \dots, \ell\}$ is the order, and the orthonormal basis functions are defined via associated Legendre polynomials $P_\ell^m$:
\begin{equation}
    Y_\ell^m(\theta, \lambda) = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi} \frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}} P_\ell^m(\cos \theta) e^{i m \lambda}.
\end{equation}
Continuous convolution on $\mathbb{S}^2$ with an invariant kernel $\kappa$ is realized through spherical harmonic transforms $\mathcal{SHT}$ and inverse transforms $\mathcal{ISHT}$:
\begin{equation}
    (\kappa \star_{\mathbb{S}^2} u)(\theta, \lambda) = \mathcal{ISHT}\left( \mathbf{W}_\ell \cdot \mathcal{SHT}(u)(\ell, m) \right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_\ell \in \mathbb{C}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ denotes degree-dependent weight tensors. By the convolution theorem on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, this spherical harmonic formulation provides exact rotational equivariance, eliminates high-latitude numerical artifacts, and ensures long-term spectral stability over multi-day atmospheric rollouts.

In the remote sensing and Earth observation domains, observations arrive as asynchronous, multi-spectral satellite imagery cubes, irregular in-situ sensor readings, and gridded reanalysis tensors.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SatMAE}~\cite{cong2022satmae} introduced a temporal and multi-spectral Vision Transformer trained via masked autoencoding. By decoupling spatial, temporal, and spectral positional embeddings, SatMAE processes multi-temporal revisit sequences with high masking ratios (up to 75\%), outperforming supervised baselines on land cover and crop yield estimation.
    \item \textbf{Presto}~\cite{tseng2023presto} developed a lightweight (1.2M parameters) transformer tailored for pixel-level time series. Presto natively ingests heterogeneous modalities---including Sentinel-1 SAR backscatter, Sentinel-2 multi-spectral bands, elevation, and ERA5 weather---yielding exceptional few-shot transfer performance.
    \item \textbf{Galileo}~\cite{tseng2025galileo} expanded multi-sensor pretraining across spatial-temporal scales using dual global and local contrastive objectives, enabling unified geospatial segmentation and dynamic flood mapping.
    \item \textbf{SkySense}~\cite{guo2024skysense} established a billion-scale ($1.9\text{B}$ parameter) multi-modal remote sensing foundation model. By formulating a factorized spatio-temporal cross-attention architecture, SkySense jointly pretrains on over 21.5 million multi-modal satellite temporal sequences (combining optical multi-spectral reflectance with synthetic aperture radar SAR backscatter). It establishes state-of-the-art generalist performance across 16 diverse Earth observation benchmarks, including fine-grained crop classification, building damage assessment, and temporal change detection.
    \item \textbf{DOFA}~\cite{xiong2024dofa} introduced a Dynamic One-For-All foundation architecture inspired by neural plasticity. Recognizing that Earth observation platforms possess disparate band wavelengths and ground sampling distances (GSD), DOFA conditions dynamic weight hypernetworks directly on continuous physical wavelengths $\lambda \in [400\text{ nm}, 14{,}000\text{ nm}]$, unifying optical, SAR, and hyperspectral temporal modalities under a single pre-trained representation.
    \item \textbf{GEO-Bench}~\cite{lacoste2023geobench} established a standardized benchmark suite for Earth monitoring foundation models. Unifying multi-temporal Sentinel-1/2, SPOT, and m-CASIS data across regional land-use segmentation and multi-spectral classification, GEO-Bench provides rigorous zero-shot and fine-tuning evaluation protocols for multi-sensor satellite representations.
    \item \textbf{EarthPT}~\cite{smith2023earthpt} developed a 700M-parameter autoregressive decoder foundation model specifically designed for Earth Observation time series. Treating multi-spectral reflectance measurements (400--2300~nm) across Copernicus Sentinel-2 pixel-year revisit sequences as dense temporal tokens, EarthPT predicts future surface reflectance evolutions with high physical fidelity.
    \item \textbf{Prithvi}~\cite{jakubik2023prithvi} and its weather-climate counterpart \textbf{Prithvi WxC}~\cite{schmude2024prithviwxc} established open-source geospatial foundation backbones jointly developed by NASA and IBM, scaling to 2.3 billion parameters.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Formal Alignment Paradigms across Heterogeneous Geometries}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:multimodal_alignment_arch}, cross-modal integration across disparate spatial geometries follows three dominant architectural paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strategy A: Early Heterogeneous Patch Projection}: Given heterogeneous modality inputs $\mathbf{X}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_m \times C_m \times H_m \times W_m}$ with disparate channel definitions and Ground Sampling Distances (GSD), modality-specific linear or convolutional patch embedding kernels $\mathbf{E}^{(m)}$ project local spatial-temporal patches into a shared hidden dimension $D$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{h}_i^{(m)} = \mathbf{E}^{(m)} \mathbf{x}_i^{(m)} + \mathbf{PE}(x_i, y_i, z_i, t_i),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathbf{PE}(\cdot)$ denotes a unified continuous 4D Fourier spatio-temporal positional encoding, ensuring coordinate harmonization across disparate satellite swaths and ground station coordinates (utilized in SatMAE~\cite{cong2022satmae} and Presto~\cite{tseng2023presto}).
    \item \textbf{Strategy B: Latent Cross-Attention Bottlenecks}: When observation token counts $N_{\text{obs}} = \sum_m N_m$ fluctuate dramatically due to dynamic sensor availability or dense planetary grids, feeding all tokens directly into full self-attention scales quadratically as $O(N_{\text{obs}}^2)$. To circumvent this computational explosion, Perceiver-style architectures (Aurora~\cite{bodnar2024aurora}, SkySense~\cite{guo2024skysense}) introduce a fixed bank of learnable latent query tokens $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ll N_{\text{obs}}$) that attend to the irregular multi-sensor input tokens via cross-attention:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{Z}_{\text{aligned}} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Z} \mathbf{W}_Q (\mathbf{H}_{\text{obs}} \mathbf{W}_K)^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right) (\mathbf{H}_{\text{obs}} \mathbf{W}_V),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathbf{H}_{\text{obs}} = [\mathbf{H}^{(1)}; \dots; \mathbf{H}^{(K)}]$ concatenates all available sensor tokens. The spatio-temporal foundation backbone then processes only the constant-size latent state $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{aligned}}$ with linear complexity in input resolution, decoupling observation density from computation.
    \item \textbf{Strategy C: Dynamic Wavelength Hypernetworks}: To support arbitrary spectral bands without modifying network weights, continuous wavelength hypernetworks (DOFA~\cite{xiong2024dofa}) encode the physical central wavelength $\lambda$ of each spectral channel via harmonic Fourier embeddings $\mathbf{e}_\lambda = [\sin(2^k \pi \lambda), \cos(2^k \pi \lambda)]_{k=0}^{K-1}$. A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) hypernetwork $\mathcal{H}_\phi$ maps $\mathbf{e}_\lambda$ to generate the convolution or linear projection weights dynamically:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{W}_{\text{patch}}(\lambda) = \mathcal{H}_\phi(\mathbf{e}_\lambda) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times (P^2 \cdot C_{\text{in}})}.
    \end{equation}
    This enables zero-shot sensor transfer across optical (e.g., Sentinel-2 10m/20m bands), thermal infrared (Landsat-8/TIRS), and radar backscatter (Sentinel-1 VV/VH) modalities within a unified model.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Neural Data Assimilation and Sensor-to-Grid Variational Inversion}
\label{sec:neural_data_assimilation}
A fundamental gap separating operational numerical weather forecasting from purely data-driven foundation models is \textit{data assimilation} (DA): the mathematical process of synthesizing sparse, asynchronous, and noisy physical observations into a physically consistent gridded initial atmospheric or oceanic state $\mathbf{x}_0$. While existing foundation models (e.g., Pangu-Weather, GraphCast) rely entirely on pre-computed gridded reanalyses (ERA5 or operational IFS analyses) produced by supercomputing NWP centers, emerging architectures introduce differentiable variational and score-based neural assimilation solvers to invert raw sensor streams directly into gridded model initial states.

\subsubsection{Variational Formulation and Neural 4D-Var}
In classical numerical weather prediction, four-dimensional variational data assimilation (4D-Var) optimizes an initial state $\mathbf{x}_0$ over an assimilation window $[t_0, t_K]$ to minimize a penalized least-squares cost function:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{J}(\mathbf{x}_0) = \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{x}_b\|_{\mathbf{B}^{-1}}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=0}^{K} \|\mathbf{y}_k - \mathcal{H}_k(\mathcal{M}_{0 \to k}(\mathbf{x}_0))\|_{\mathbf{R}_k^{-1}}^2,
    \label{eq:classical_4dvar}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_b$ is the background prior state, $\mathbf{B}$ is the background error covariance matrix, $\mathbf{y}_k$ denotes sparse observations at time $t_k$, $\mathbf{R}_k$ is the observation error covariance, $\mathcal{H}_k$ is the observation forward operator, and $\mathcal{M}_{0 \to k}$ is the non-linear dynamical forward model. In operational NWP, minimizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:classical_4dvar} requires maintaining complex adjoint code $\mathcal{M}^*$ to compute gradients $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_0} \mathcal{J}$, consuming up to 80\% of operational supercomputing compute budgets.

To overcome this bottleneck, Fablet et al.~\cite{fablet2021learning} introduced \textbf{4DVarNet}, reformulating variational data assimilation as an end-to-end learnable gradient-based solver. 4DVarNet replaces the analytical minimization loop with an unrolled recurrent neural architecture:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_0^{(i+1)} = \mathbf{x}_0^{(i)} - \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_\theta \left( \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_0} \mathcal{J}(\mathbf{x}_0^{(i)}), \mathbf{h}^{(i)} \right),
    \label{eq:neural_4dvar_step}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_\theta$ is a learned recurrent neural operator (e.g., ConvLSTM or U-Net solver), $\mathbf{h}^{(i)}$ represents internal solver memory states, and the gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_0} \mathcal{J}$ is computed via automated differentiation through the observation operator $\mathcal{H}_k$ and neural forward dynamics $\mathcal{M}_\phi$. Originally deployed for sea surface height (SSH) reconstruction from sparse satellite altimetry tracks in oceanography, 4DVarNet achieves orders-of-magnitude faster convergence than classical conjugate gradient descent while learning dynamic background error covariances directly from historical data.

Scaling neural data assimilation to global atmospheric forecasting, Xiao et al.~\cite{xiao2023fengwu4dvar} introduced \textbf{FengWu-4DVar}. FengWu-4DVar couples the FengWu multi-scale atmospheric transformer with an unrolled 4D variational optimization loop. By exploiting the end-to-end differentiability of the neural forecasting backbone, FengWu-4DVar directly backpropagates observation residuals from asynchronous in-situ soundings and satellite radiances through 24-hour assimilation windows, generating initialized atmospheric states that match operational ECMWF IFS analysis quality while reducing assimilation wall-clock time from hours to seconds.

\subsubsection{Score-Based and Diffusion Data Assimilation}
Complementing deterministic variational solvers, generative diffusion models reframe data assimilation as conditional posterior sampling: drawing physical state trajectories $\mathbf{x}_{0:K} \sim p(\mathbf{x}_{0:K} \mid \mathbf{y}_{0:K})$.

Rozet and Louppe~\cite{rozet2023sda} formulated \textbf{Score-based Data Assimilation} (SDA), showing that continuous score-based stochastic differential equations can execute exact spatio-temporal smoothing and filtering over non-linear fluid dynamics without tangent-linear approximations. Given a trained unconditional foundation prior $s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) \approx \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t)$, SDA conditions the reverse-time generative SDE on sparse partial observations $\mathbf{y}$ via Tweedie's guidance:
\begin{equation}
    d\mathbf{x}_t = \left[ \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_t, t) - g(t)^2 \left( \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{x}_t) \right) \right] dt + g(t) \, d\mathbf{\bar{w}}.
    \label{eq:score_da_sde}
\end{equation}
Under Gaussian observation noise $\mathbf{y} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{R})$, the intractable conditional score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ is approximated analytically via the posterior expectation $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0(\mathbf{x}_t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}} (\mathbf{x}_t - \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t))$:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log p_t(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{x}_t) \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}} \mathbf{H}^T \left( \mathbf{H} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_t \mathbf{H}^T + \mathbf{R} \right)^{-1} \left( \mathbf{y} - \mathbf{H} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0(\mathbf{x}_t) \right).
    \label{eq:tweedie_da_gradient}
\end{equation}

Scaling diffusion assimilation to weather-scale operational state estimation, Huang et al.~\cite{huang2024diffda} introduced \textbf{DiffDA}. DiffDA trains a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) conditioned on sparse surface weather station observations and radiosonde soundings. Operating directly on physical observation coordinates, DiffDA samples ensemble analyses that accurately reproduce fine-scale frontal boundaries, atmospheric rivers, and convective storms, demonstrating that neural generative assimilation can bridge heterogeneous sensor observations directly into gridded foundation state spaces.

\subsection{Probabilistic Diffusion and Extreme-Event Ensembles}
\label{sec:diffusion_ensembles}
A pervasive theoretical limitation of deterministic forecasting models trained under mean squared error ($L_2$) or mean absolute error ($L_1$) losses is their vulnerability to \textit{spectral blurring} and variance dissipation at extended forecast horizons. Formally, given an observation history $\mathbf{y}_{1:t}$ and future atmospheric target $\mathbf{x}_{t+\tau}$, optimizing an $L_2$ regression objective $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{x}_{t+\tau} - f_\theta(\mathbf{y}_{1:t})\|^2]$ provably converges to the conditional posterior mean:
\begin{equation}
    f^*_\theta(\mathbf{y}_{1:t}) = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{t+\tau} \mid \mathbf{y}_{1:t}] = \int \mathbf{x} \, p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{y}_{1:t}) \, d\mathbf{x}.
\end{equation}
Because atmospheric flow exhibits non-linear chaotic dynamics with positive Lyapunov exponents, the true posterior probability density $p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{y}_{1:t})$ bifurcates into multi-modal trajectories at extended lead times ($\tau \ge 5$ days). Averaging over these distinct physical realizations washes out high-wavenumber turbulent eddies, severely dampens the kinetic energy power spectrum $E(k) \propto k^{-5/3}$ or $k^{-3}$, and underestimates extreme-event tail probabilities $P(\|\mathbf{x}\| > \tau_{\text{threshold}})$.

To resolve this limitation, generative diffusion models reframe weather forecasting as stochastic sampling from the true conditional distribution $p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{y})$.

\subsubsection{Continuous Score-Based Diffusion Formulation}
Let the atmospheric state trajectory follow a continuous forward stochastic differential equation (SDE) over diffusion time $s \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    d\mathbf{x}_s = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_s, s) \, ds + g(s) \, d\mathbf{w},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w}$ is standard Brownian motion, $\mathbf{f}$ represents drift, and $g(s)$ governs noise dispersion. By Anderson's reverse-time theorem, the generative reverse trajectory is governed by:
\begin{equation}
    d\mathbf{x}_s = \left[ \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_s, s) - g(s)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_s} \log p_s(\mathbf{x}_s \mid \mathbf{y}) \right] ds + g(s) \, d\mathbf{\bar{w}},
\end{equation}
where $d\mathbf{\bar{w}}$ denotes backward Brownian motion, and the score function $s_\theta(\mathbf{x}_s, s, \mathbf{y}) \approx \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_s} \log p_s(\mathbf{x}_s \mid \mathbf{y})$ is parameterized by a deep neural operator or graph network trained via denoising score matching.

\subsubsection{GenCast, SEEDS, and FuXi-Extreme: Operational Diffusion Ensembling}
Price et al.~\cite{price2023gencast} operationalized this paradigm in \textbf{GenCast}, conditioning a score-matching network on spherical multi-mesh graphs using historical 6-hour atmospheric increments $(\mathbf{x}_{t-6\text{h}}, \mathbf{x}_t)$. By sampling 50 stochastic trajectories, GenCast accurately generates medium-range ensemble forecasts that maintain realistic energy spectra up to Day 15, outperforming ECMWF ENS in Continuous Ranked Probability Scores (CRPS) across over 80\% of headline atmospheric variables and drastically improving tropical cyclone track and intensity predictions.

Concurrently, Li et al.~\cite{li2024seeds} introduced \textbf{SEEDS} (Scalable Ensemble Envelope Diffusion Sampler), demonstrating that generative diffusion can emulate operational numerical weather forecast ensembles (such as NOAA GEFS) conditioned on only 1 or 2 deterministic forecast trajectories. SEEDS generates large ensembles (e.g., 50 members) at $1^\circ$ operational resolution orders of magnitude faster than numerical ensembles, precisely reproducing the spread-skill relationship and capturing extreme heatwave and storm tail events without numerical energy collapse.

Extending diffusion specifically to hazard extremes, Zhong et al.~\cite{chen2023fuxiextreme} formulated \textbf{FuXi-Extreme}. While deterministic weather models drastically underestimate tail precipitation rates and gale-force wind gusts, FuXi-Extreme couples a cascade Swin Transformer with a conditional latent diffusion model. Trained on high-resolution ERA5 extreme events, it generates realistic sharp spatial gradients for extreme precipitation ($>50\text{ mm/day}$) and 10-meter wind gusts, resolving the longstanding spectral smoothing dilemma for operational disaster early warnings.

\subsection{Universal Tokenization and Cross-Domain Temporal Transferability}
\label{sec:universal_tokenization}
A major architectural obstacle in scientific foundation models is the profound heterogeneity of physical data representations: regular lat-lon reanalysis grids (ERA5), irregular unstructured meshes (finite-volume ocean models), sparse in-situ station networks (hydrology streamflow gauges), 1D continuous high-frequency waveforms (seismology), and irregular multi-spectral satellite revisits (remote sensing). Recent research develops universal tokenization mechanisms to bridge this divide:

\subsubsection{Unified Multi-Task Architectures: UniTS}
Gao et al.~\cite{gao2024units} developed \textbf{UniTS}, a unified multi-task foundation model for time series. Unlike domain-specific architectures requiring separate parameterizations for each dataset, UniTS processes arbitrary numbers of variables and sequences by introducing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sequence-Variable Tokenization}: Decouples temporal sequence patches and variable channels into modular tokens via lightweight linear projections.
    \item \textbf{Prompt-Conditioned Task Tokens}: Ingests task specification tokens (e.g., forecasting horizon, classification labels, imputation mask) into self-attention layers, conditioning a single shared transformer backbone to execute forecasting, anomaly detection, imputation, and classification across 38 diverse datasets without architectural modification.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Language Model Tokenization for Time Series: Chronos}
Ansari et al.~\cite{ansari2024chronos} introduced \textbf{Chronos}, demonstrating that classical language model tokenization principles translate directly to continuous scientific and physical time series. Chronos discretizes real-valued time series through affine mean-variance normalization followed by uniform quantile binning into a fixed vocabulary of $V$ discrete tokens (e.g., 4096 bins). Pretrained on diverse time-series corpora using standard cross-entropy auto-regression over T5 architectures, Chronos demonstrates that language model pretraining objectives yield remarkable zero-shot probabilistic forecasting capabilities across diverse physical and socio-economic systems.

\subsubsection{Autoregressive Probabilistic Foundations: Lag-Llama}
Rasul et al.~\cite{rasul2024lagllama} formulated \textbf{Lag-Llama}, a dedicated probabilistic foundation model utilizing a decoder-only LLaMA-style transformer architecture. Ingesting historical lagged temporal features and tokenized continuous values, Lag-Llama applies rotary position embeddings (RoPE) and outputs parametric Student-$t$ distribution parameters $(\mu, \sigma, \nu)$ at each step. This formulation natively captures fat-tailed physical distributions and extreme deviations across physical sensor streams.

\subsubsection{Open Foundation Models: MOMENT}
Goswami et al.~\cite{goswami2024moment} introduced \textbf{MOMENT}, an open-source family of foundation models trained on the \textit{Time-series Pile}, a curated corpus of over 13 million diverse sequences spanning earth science sensors, industrial machinery, and physical processes. Using a patched T5-style masked autoencoder architecture, MOMENT establishes consistent zero-shot and few-shot transfer capabilities across diverse scientific temporal benchmarks.

\subsubsection{Atmospheric Patch Randomization: Stormer}
Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2023stormer} introduced \textbf{Stormer}, demonstrating that a standard Vision Transformer backbone can match or exceed specialized weather forecasting architectures when coupled with two critical physical inductive biases:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Randomized Dynamics Objective}: During pretraining, Stormer forecasts atmospheric states across randomized lead times $\Delta t \sim \mathcal{U}(6\text{h}, 120\text{h})$, forcing the model to learn multi-scale continuous temporal dynamics rather than overfitting to fixed autoregressive step sizes.
    \item \textbf{Pressure-Weighted Vertical Loss}: Weighs atmospheric layer losses proportional to geopotential layer thickness and density, aligning optimization with physical mass distribution.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Standardized Waveform Tokenization: SeisBench}
In geophysics, Woollam et al.~\cite{woollam2022seisbench} established \textbf{SeisBench}, a standardized framework and benchmarking suite unifying 3-component continuous seismic waveform datasets (ETHZ, GEOFON, INSTANCE, SCEDC, STEAD, comprising over 30 million waveforms). By standardizing sampling rates ($100\text{ Hz}$), coordinate rotations, and trace tokenization, SeisBench enables cross-regional foundation pretraining and transferability for phase arrival picking, earthquake detection, and fault rupture localization.

\subsection{Physical Conservation Constraints and Differentiable Hybrid Solvers}
\label{sec:physics_conservation}
A persistent vulnerability of purely data-driven black-box neural networks is their tendency to violate fundamental physical conservation principles over prolonged autoregressive rollouts, leading to unphysical mass dissipation, kinetic energy damping, or moisture divergence. Recent advances address this through distinct levels of physical integration:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Soft Physics Regularization}: Incorporates auxiliary loss penalties that penalize physical inconsistencies during training:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} + \lambda_{\text{phys}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{conservation}},
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{conservation}}$ penalizes deviations in global total dry mass, vertical geostrophic balance, or spectral kinetic energy distributions (employed in FourCastNet~\cite{pathak2022fourcastnet}, FengWu~\cite{chen2023fengwu}, and Stormer~\cite{nguyen2023stormer}).
    \item \textbf{Hard Architectural Invariance}: Enforces conservation laws and symmetry directly into neural layer transformations. For instance, SFNO~\cite{bonev2023sfno} guarantees $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ rotational equivariance on the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^2$, preventing polar singularities and high-latitude numerical distortions by construction.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Multiscale Subgrid Parameterization: ClimSim}: Yu et al.~\cite{yu2023climsim} established \textbf{ClimSim}, the largest multiscale dataset and benchmark for training deep learning subgrid physics emulators. In multiscale climate modeling (e.g., E3SM-MMF), macro-scale fluid dynamics are integrated by numerical PDE solvers on coarse grids, while complex micro-scale processes (moist convection, cloud formation, turbulence, and radiative transfer) are parameterized by neural networks. ClimSim provides 5.7 billion high-resolution atmospheric column profiles, benchmarking physics-informed ML architectures on strict conservation of total column water mass and dry static energy.
    \item \textbf{Differentiable Hybrid Solvers: NeuralGCM}: Kochkov et al.~\cite{kochkov2024neuralgcm} coupled a fully differentiable atmospheric dynamical core with neural network parameterizations. The resolved large-scale hydrodynamics are integrated explicitly by an analytical spectral PDE solver that strictly enforces conservation of mass, momentum, and moisture:
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\partial \mathbf{X}}{\partial t} = \mathcal{F}_{\text{dynamical}}(\mathbf{X}) + \mathcal{N}_{\theta}(\mathbf{X}),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dynamical}}$ executes analytical fluid dynamics, while the neural network $\mathcal{N}_{\theta}$ parameterizes sub-grid physical processes. NeuralGCM demonstrates unprecedented multi-decadal stability without climate drift, matching the operational deterministic accuracy of ECMWF IFS HRES at 1--10 day weather horizons while preserving global physical conservation laws for decadal climate simulations.
\end{enumerate}
